## Scope

Single-page cinematic experience. Experience mode: visitors enter a NERV synchronization apparatus, move through seven spatial chapters, read both cast factions in first-appearance order, trigger Japanese voice lines, and release the AT field.

## Audience and job

Evangelion fans arriving from a social link want atmosphere first and readable character detail second. They must be able to identify the work immediately, browse without sound, understand the fan-made status, and reach every supplied character and solitude line without being trapped by the spectacle.

## Direction contract

THESIS: A scrollable synchronization observation film, where the visitor passes through a closing AT field, six animated archival spaces, and a final release instead of browsing a static fan dossier.

OWN-WORLD: Near black and warm paper lit by warning red and emergency orange; acid green and Eva-01 purple appear only as machine states, blue only for moonlight and Tokyo-3 night. Giant Mincho Japanese, Bodoni English titles, narrow instrument labels, and Source Serif reading copy. Cel-image depth cards, procedural hangar geometry, octahedral AT fields, screening-room masks, and civil-defence registers replace generic neon HUD glass.

STORY: The visitor starts synchronization, meets the NERV and antagonist casts by first appearance, inspects four machines through depth scans, neutralizes an Angel field, watches Tokyo-3 rise from the Geofront, crosses two eras of records, then breaks the same field and lands on a quiet white release card.

SIGNATURE INTERACTION: Scroll drives the camera through a live WebGL AT-field aperture; at the Angel chapter, click or Enter neutralizes the field, physically fracturing its geometry and revealing the profile beneath before the final field re-forms and dissolves.

FIRST VIEWPORT: A full-viewport red octahedral field encloses the camera over black. Oversized Japanese `新世纪福音战士 / 同步协议` sits behind it at architectural scale; the title block and two explicit actions, `开始同步` and `静音浏览`, occupy the lower-left; a sync readout occupies the lower-right; the first fragment of the cast line-up remains visible at the bottom edge.
